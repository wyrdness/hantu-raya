## Overview

Hantu Raya, literally 'Great Ghost' or 'High Spirit', is one of the most powerful familiar spirits in Malay folklore and folk-Islamic practice. Unlike ordinary hantu, it is bound through pact to a human master — historically a bomoh (shaman) or pawang (specialist practitioner) — and serves as a supernatural double that performs labour, provides protection, and can act as an alibi for its master's whereabouts. Folktale descriptions give it a humanoid form covered in black hair save for the face, with rough grey skin, long sharp teeth, and red eyes; in service it most often takes on the appearance of its master. The pact requires regular offerings of ancak — a ritual meal traditionally consisting of yellow glutinous rice, eggs, roasted chicken, rice flakes, and a doll — failing which the spirit may turn on its master or its master's family. Hantu Raya is frequently inherited as hantu pusaka, descending through a lineage and binding successive generations to the offerings until it is ritually released. The figure is treated as problematic by orthodox Islamic authorities in Malaysia but persists strongly in popular belief and is a recurring presence in modern Malaysian horror cinema, most prominently the 2007 film Jangan Pandang Belakang and its 2009 sequel.

- **Origin**: Malay: hantu ('ghost' or 'spirit') + raya ('great', 'high', 'major').
- **Meaning**: Literally 'Great Ghost' — denoting power, not necessarily kingship over other spirits.
- **First use**: Documented in 20th-century Malaysian ethnographies; full-scale academic treatment in Zain Tajudin's 1991 paper for the Journal of the Siam Society.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Bound familiar / pact spirit** _(Bomoh and pawang practitioners, Malay folk-religious tradition)_

Within Malay folk practice, Hantu Raya is a real spirit bound by oath to a human master in exchange for offerings, performing labour and protection.

### Skeptical

**Sociological function in village politics** _(Malaysian folklore studies)_

Folklorists and anthropologists read the Hantu Raya as encoding the social authority of the bomoh/pawang and the moral economy of ritual obligation in rural Malay villages.

**Theological objection (orthodox Islam)** _(Malaysian Islamic religious authorities)_

Mainstream Malaysian Islamic authorities consider the binding of spirits problematic or shirk-adjacent and discourage the practice while not necessarily denying the existence of jinn-like entities.
